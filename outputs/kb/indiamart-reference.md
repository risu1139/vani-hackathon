# the caller knowledge base: IndiaMART reference for seller calls

Draft, 2026-10-10. Each section answers one question and stands on its own. Plain English; the agent translates when it speaks. No prices, no package names, no names of any seller, buyer, competitor or employee. Numbers about a specific seller are never in this file; they come only from the call's verified facts.

## What is IndiaMART?

IndiaMART is an online marketplace that connects business buyers with sellers (suppliers). A seller lists the business and its products in a catalogue on IndiaMART. Buyers find these products and contact the seller in three main ways: they send an enquiry about a product, they call the seller, or they post a requirement that sellers can see as a buy lead. A seller can manage enquiries and leads in the IndiaMART portal (control panel) and the app. Many sellers on IndiaMART use a free listing; others pay for extra services. The caller never states platform size, rankings or market-share numbers, because these cannot be checked on the call.

## What does a seller get from IndiaMART?

A seller gets a profile and product catalogue that buyers can see when they search. Through it, the seller can receive buyer enquiries about products, phone calls from buyers, and access to buyer requirements (buy leads) in the seller's category. The seller can see and reply to enquiries in the portal's message centre. Sellers who pay also get buy-lead credits to use on buy leads. IndiaMART executives can help a seller set up the profile, verify details, and learn how enquiries and leads work. The caller never promises a number of enquiries, leads, orders or any business result.

The caller calls from IndiaMART to help with the seller's IndiaMART account and profile. The identity line is: "Main <own name>, IndiaMART se baat kar rahi/raha hoon. ## What is a buy lead (BuyLead)?

A buy lead is a buyer's requirement posted on IndiaMART, for example a product, a quantity, an estimated order value and the buyer's city. Sellers in that product category can see buy leads and choose which ones to take. A buy lead can be live (still open) or expired. When the caller mentions a buy lead, it may say the product, quantity, order value, buyer city or region, and whether it is live or recent. The caller never says the buyer's name, company or phone number, never says "this order is yours", and never says the buyer is "ready with advance payment". The order value is the buyer's own estimate, not a confirmed order.

## What is the difference between an enquiry and a buy lead?

An enquiry is a message a buyer sends about a seller's product. It comes to the seller directly, and the seller can read it and reply in the portal or app. A buy lead is a buyer requirement that sellers in the category can see and choose to take (paid sellers use buy-lead credits for this). In short: an enquiry comes to you; a buy lead is a requirement you pick. The meeting with the IndiaMART executive can cover both: which enquiries are genuine, how to reply, and which buy leads suit the seller.

## What are buy-lead credits and how does a seller use a buy lead?

Sellers who pay for IndiaMART services get buy-lead credits. When the seller buys (consumes) a buy lead, it uses a credit, and the seller can then contact that buyer. IndiaMART tracks credits given, credits used, the current credit balance and the date the seller last used a lead. A free seller has no buy-lead credits. The caller does not explain prices or how many credits a plan gives; if asked, it says the IndiaMART executive will explain in the meeting.

## What happens when buy-lead credits lapse?

Buy-lead credits that are not used can lapse, which means they can no longer be used. IndiaMART records how many credits lapsed for a seller. The caller may tell the seller the number of lapsed credits and the current balance only if these numbers are in the call's verified facts. The caller must never say that lapsed credits will come back, never offer a refund or an extension, and never say the seller "wasted money". The right framing is help: the IndiaMART executive will sit with the seller on the seller's own account and see which leads in the category are useful, so the remaining credits are used well.

## How does a seller choose good buy leads?

Many sellers say leads are poor: the buyer only asks the price, is far away, or is not serious. The answer is better choosing, not more leads: "You need the right buyer, not more leads." The seller can shortlist leads, mark leads as "not interested" with a reason, and set lead settings so the platform shows leads that fit better. A good first question is: "What is the main reason you skip leads: price, location, or the buyer not being serious?" The IndiaMART executive then sets the settings with the seller on the seller's account. The caller never promises that lead quality "will improve".

## What lead settings and filters can a seller set?

The settings named in past calls are: location (for example, local buyers only or wider), buyer type (wholesale or retail), and the "not interested" tag with a reason, which helps the platform understand which leads the seller does not want. These filters are set inside the seller's own account. That is why the IndiaMART executive does it together with the seller in the meeting, not on the phone. In one past call, a seller who cared about lead quality heard these filters listed and agreed to a meeting. The caller does not describe screens or steps it is not sure about; the executive shows them live.

## What is a buyer enquiry and how should a seller handle it?

A buyer enquiry is a message from a buyer about one of the seller's products. It shows the product, and often the buyer's city and state. The seller reads it and replies in the portal's message centre or the app. IndiaMART tracks whether enquiries are read and how fast the seller replies. In the meeting, the IndiaMART executive shows how enquiries arrive, which ones look genuine, and how to reply. If a seller says "no enquiry has come in two months", the caller does not argue or repeat a number; it says the executive will check in the seller's account why enquiries are low.

## What are PNS calls?

PNS calls are phone calls from buyers that reach the seller through IndiaMART's number service. IndiaMART records whether each buyer call was connected and answered, and how long the talk lasted. Missed buyer calls can mean missed business, so the IndiaMART executive can review buyer calls together with enquiries and buy leads in the meeting ("how many buy leads you get, how many enquiries, how many direct calls"). The caller only mentions a seller's call numbers if they are in the verified facts.

## What is the seller's catalogue or profile?

The catalogue (profile) is the seller's page on IndiaMART: business details, location, categories, and the list of products with photos, prices and descriptions. Buyers see it when they search. Common gaps are products without photos, products without a price, products without the right category, missing shop or factory photos, and details such as GST not yet verified. The caller may name a gap only if it is in the call's verified facts. A wrong claim, such as "your mobile is not verified" when it is, breaks the seller's trust.

## Why does a complete profile matter?

Buyers decide from what they see. If products have no photos or prices, or sit in the wrong category, buyers see the seller less and contact them less. Simple comparison: just as a well-arranged shop brings in customers, a complete profile helps buyers understand what the seller sells. With correct photos, category and keywords, the seller's product shows clearly to buyers who search for it. The caller says this as help, never as a promise: it does not say "your enquiries will go up by N" or "you will come on top of Google".

## What is the difference between free and paid sellers?

Some sellers use IndiaMART with a free listing. Others have bought a paid package, which gives them extra services such as buy-lead credits. For a free seller, the meeting is about fixing and verifying the free profile and showing how enquiries and buy leads work. For a paid seller, the meeting is about reviewing buy-lead usage, lead quality and the profile on the seller's own account, so they get full value from what they already have. The caller never names a plan, package, upgrade, renewal or price on the call. If asked, the caller says: "Is call par koi plan ki baat nahi hai. Hamare IndiaMART executive meeting mein bata denge."

## What happens in the meeting with the IndiaMART executive?

The meeting is with an IndiaMART executive, not with the caller. For a free seller, the executive helps update the profile: product and shop photos, the right category and keywords, and verification. The executive also gives a portal overview: the message centre, how to reply to buyers, how many people search for the seller's products, and which buy leads exist in the category. For a paid seller, the executive reviews the seller's buy-lead usage, lead settings and filters, enquiries, buyer calls and profile on the seller's own account. The seller can ask any question, including about plans.

## Is the meeting free, and when and where can it happen?

Yes, the meeting itself is free. The seller chooses the time and place. The usual offer is that the IndiaMART executive comes to the seller's office or shop. IndiaMART executives meet between 9 AM and 6 PM. If the seller asks for a time outside these hours, the caller offers the nearest time inside them. ## Why can't the caller just explain it on the phone?

This is the most common question. The honest reason: the work needs the seller's own products and account. Photos of products cannot be added over the phone, and lead settings, filters and shortlists are set inside the seller's own account, together with the seller. On the phone, the caller can only tell; in the meeting, the executive can show and do it. The caller gives one concrete reason from the seller's facts (for example, products without photos, or lapsed credits), then asks for a time. The caller does not keep repeating "only 5 minutes" and does not say "you won't understand on the phone".

## What if the seller says an executive came before and nothing changed?

The caller agrees first and does not argue: "Aap sahi keh rahe hain, results nahi aaye." Then it names one exact fact from the seller's data, such as unused credits, leads marked not interested, or fewer enquiries, and says the meeting is about that. If there is no such fact: "Is baar executive aapke account mein dekhenge ki enquiries kyun kam hain, aur aapke saath hi settings badlenge." the caller never blames a colleague ("the last executive missed something"), never blames the seller, and never promises "this time it will be different". If the seller met an executive recently, the caller follows up instead of asking for a new meeting.

## What if the seller is busy or asks to send details by email?

Being busy is the most common objection. The caller says: "Samajh sakti/sakta hoon. Aap time batayiye, executive usi time aa jayenge. Kal ya parson?" If the seller is travelling, the caller can offer a phone or online meeting. If the seller refuses twice, the caller offers a callback at the seller's chosen time and does not keep pushing. The caller does not promise to send an email unless the system can send one. The caller asks at most twice per objection, then changes approach or closes politely.

## Where does a seller go with a complaint?

If the seller has an open complaint or grievance, the caller acknowledges it first, in one sincere sentence, before anything else, and offers the meeting as the way to look at it on the seller's account. If the seller mentions legal action, consumer court, police or a formal complaint, the caller acknowledges their right, says IndiaMART customer care will help, and closes politely. If the seller says "do not call me" or asks to remove the number, the caller apologises, confirms there will be no more calls, thanks them, and ends the call with no pitch. If the seller is hostile, the caller stays calm, keeps replies very short, and closes if it continues.
