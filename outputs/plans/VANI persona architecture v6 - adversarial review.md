# VANI persona architecture v6: adversarial review

Sources: [[VANI persona architecture v6]], `raw/hackathon/2026-10-08-ps04-persona-design-problem-doc.md`, `raw/hackathon/2026-10-07-hackathon-deck-problem-statements.md`, team decisions `raw/hackathon/2026-10-0{8,9}-team-decision*.md`, `raw/research/2026-10-0{8,9}-*.md`, `app/spike/code_tools/vani_onstart_test.py`, `app/contracts/seller_context.schema.json`.

Reviewer stance: hackathon judge + senior architect. Written 2026-10-09 (day 1). The binding team decisions are taken as given. They are attacked only where the plan fails to implement them or where they need a mitigation.

## Verdict: PASS WITH FIXES (5 blockers)

The pre-call half is strong and mostly proven. OnStart sets voice, pace, pitch and the opening per call, and `call_plan` survives the turns. The live half is weaker than the plan claims. The in-Sarvam switcher that actually runs (`ReadSellerSignal`) has no "interest" signal. It does not log language switches. Its switch log has no "change" or "reason" field and silently resets once it grows past 4,000 characters. The composed tone, formality and Hinglish mix have no Sarvam variable to carry them, so they are lost after the first seller turn. The closing line "no gap remains vs PS04" is therefore false on Switches 25, D4 and Distinctness 20. Two process risks could zero the submission: code written on 7–8 Oct (before the event) against the deck's "all build work happens during the 2 days" rule, and organiser rulings that are still open (frontier decider vs "core agent must be Sarvam"; off-premises models vs "no customer data leaves the premises"). The body of the plan is also stale: the diagram, B8, §5, §8 and the risks still describe an external signal server, Jev next turn and open tests that the appended update has closed. All of this can be fixed in hours. None of it needs a rule table or a fallback persona.
