# Translate seller moods into VANI's voice

## Summary in simple words

1. In this report, "persona" means VANI's persona: its voice, speed, tone, language and call plan. Seller data is the input.
2. A rushed seller is often not rushed on the next call. In our past data, 31 of 100 rushed sellers were rushed again. The normal rate is 21 of 100. So about 7 of 10 rushed sellers are calm on the next call.
3. Thus, do not lock VANI into one style before the call. Set a safe start. Prepare backup moves in the plan. Make VANI react faster when the seller's history shows the risk.
4. Confusion is short-term. Its effect is mostly gone after a few days. "Not interested" also fades. Its effect drops by about two thirds in 28 days.
5. Voice and speed cannot change during a Sarvam call. Wording, length, tone, language and plan can change. Put strong guesses only into things that can change.
6. Start with six VANI personas. Each persona is a fixed mix of voice, speed, formality, warmth, length and plan style. Each one is one published Sarvam agent version.
7. The old VANI calls used one voice and one style. Thus, every "this seller gets this persona" rule is a guess at the start. It becomes evidence only when VANI tries different personas and logs the results.
8. Keep a score table: seller type × VANI persona → calls and meetings. Keep, tune, drop or add personas from this table.
9. To see a 3-point gain in meetings, you need about 2,000 calls per persona per seller type.
10. Do not use seller gender, religion, caste or name as input. Give each persona a male and a female voice. Test both. Do not pick the voice gender from a stereotype.

**Seller behaviour should set VANI's starting odds and reflexes, not a fixed character.** PS07, IndiaMART's archive of 269,455 answered VANI calls, shows that "rushed", "terse" and "English-heavy" recur above base rate at every gap, so they act like weak traits. Rush recurs in **30.8% of next calls against a 20.8% base**, which means **about 69% of prior rushers are not rushed next time**. Confusion is a short-lived state (34.3% vs 7.7% within 3 days, 14.3% vs 5.5% at 4–30 days), and a "Not Interested" outcome decays toward base with a **time constant of about 28 days** (half-life ≈ 20 days). The right translation therefore has three layers: a robust pre-call default for the settings Sarvam fixes per call (voice and pace), contingency branches written into the call plan that is re-sent every turn, and live-switch thresholds that drop when the prior is high. The library of VANI agent personas follows the industry shape that Alexa+ adopted in 2026, a few named styles defined as levels on shared dimensions over one fixed fact layer. Six archetypes map onto six published Sarvam agent versions. The binding caveat is that **PS07 had one VANI style**, so every seller→style mapping in this report starts as theory-grounded assumption. It becomes evidence only once VANI logs deliberate variation, roughly **2,000 calls per arm per seller segment** to detect a 3-point meeting lift. Until then, the honest claim is "we know which sellers behave how; we infer what VANI should do".

Evidence tags used throughout: **PROVEN** = measured by us on PS07 aggregates or in a spike. **DOCUMENTED** = stated by a cited paper, official document or our `raw/` sources. **ASSUMED** = our design or inference, not yet tested. Numbers from PS07 are aggregates only. They come from our between-run notes ([persona learning notes](../research_notes/Between%20run%20learning%20loops/persona_learning.md)) and the library prototype ([PS07 library prototype](../research_notes/Data%20derived%20persona%20library/ps07_library_prototype.md)).

## Seven in ten prior rushers arrive calm, so history sets odds, not identity

The trait-versus-state question decides the whole design, and PS07 answers it more cleanly than any outside source. On consecutive calls to the same seller, **rush recurs at 30.8% against a 20.8% base (1.48×, n = 276)**. **Terse replies recur at 41.0% vs 17.8% (n = 183)** and **English-heavy speech at 13.8% vs 4.3% (n = 65)**. All three stay elevated at every gap length. Rush, for example, is 28.2% vs 19.4% within 3 days, 35.9% vs 23.3% at 4–30 days and 25.0% vs 17.1% beyond 30 days. That is the signature of a stable lean, not a passing mood (PROVEN, [persona learning notes](../research_notes/Between%20run%20learning%20loops/persona_learning.md)). But the lean is weak. A prior rusher is only 10 points more likely than average to rush again, so **roughly seven in ten arrive unhurried**. A persona that hard-codes "this seller is rushed" mis-serves the majority of the sellers it targets.

Confusion behaves differently. It recurs at **25.0% vs 7.0% overall (n = 68)**, but the excess sits almost entirely in the first few days: **34.3% vs 7.7% within 3 days, falling to 14.3% vs 5.5% at 4–30 days** (PROVEN, small n). Outcomes fade the same way. A "Not Interested" call is followed by another NI **78.3% of the time within 3 days, 65.5% at 4–14 days, 54.7% at 15–45 days and 41.3% beyond 45 days**, which is the first-call base of 41.1%. Fitting the excess gives an exponential **time constant τ ≈ 28 days, a half-life of about 20 days** (PROVEN counts on n = 57,440; ASSUMED fit). Meeting-fixed history shows no visible decay (~21–22% repeat at every longer gap), so a good relationship persists while a bad mood fades.

Two further PS07 facts limit what any pre-call persona can claim. First, **pre-call data barely forecasts in-call behaviour**: rush, terse, confusion and interest are predicted at AUC 0.52–0.58 by profile, persona or full features alike. English-heavy speech is the one exception, at AUC 0.68 from profile (PROVEN, [PS07 library prototype](../research_notes/Data%20derived%20persona%20library/ps07_library_prototype.md)). Second, **prior rush carries no outcome penalty** (next-call meeting 14.1% vs 15.0%), while prior frustration and "are you a bot?" questions do (5.1% and 5.7% vs ~15%, n = 39/35) (PROVEN, weak). History is far more useful for outcomes than for moods: the previous call predicts the next meeting at AUC 0.71 against 0.58 for profile, and the next NI at 0.78 against 0.54 (PROVEN).

Outside research adds a twist. Users talk to bots **14.5% less politely and formally and 5.3% less fluently** than to human agents ([Zhang & Yu, arXiv 2510.02645](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.02645v1)). Part of what PS07 labels "terse" is therefore a bot-directed register, not a fixed personal trait (ASSUMED). Speakers also drift toward a system's speech rate without noticing ([KTH, ICPhS 2003](https://www.speech.kth.se/~jocke/publications/ICPhS03.pdf)), so VANI's pace partly *creates* the seller's pace rather than only following it.

The design consequence is a two-part prior per delivery dimension. Trait-like signals (rush, terse, English-heavy, bot-question) get a per-seller Beta posterior shrunk to the segment rate, `p_trait = (k + α·m_seg) / (n + α)` with α ≈ 4–10. One observed call moves the estimate partway, and two consistent calls move it most of the way. State-like signals (confusion, last outcome, callback request, frustration) get a recency weight `w = exp(−gap/28 d)` and fall back to defaults after 45–60 days (ASSUMED design, sized from PROVEN decay). Live evidence always outranks both.

## Theory gives VANI directions, while outcome mining needs variation PS07 lacks

Expert and theory-led practice turns "the customer is X" into "the agent does Y" through condition→action playbooks, each row justified by a principle. **Communication Accommodation Theory** says speakers converge toward a partner to gain approval and smooth the exchange. It also names over-accommodation, such as over-simplifying to older listeners, as a recognised failure ([Giles, Coupland & Coupland](https://people.cs.pitt.edu/%7Elitman/courses/cs3710/gilescouplandcoupland.pdf)). The evidence on speech-rate convergence specifically is weaker than early claims. Session-level convergence can vanish when measured turn by turn ([Schweitzer & Walsh 2016](https://www.ims.uni-stuttgart.de/documents/team/schweitz/docs/SchweitzerWalsh2016.pdf)). Entrainment's effect on trust **depends on the feature, the language and the voice**. An English male avatar that entrained on intensity and rate gained trust, while a Spanish/Slovak female avatar that entrained only on rate was trusted slightly *less* than a disentraining baseline ([Levitan, Columbia thesis](https://www.cs.columbia.edu/speech/ThesisFiles/rivka_levitan.pdf)). Mimicry helps only when subtle, and several human–human benefits **did not replicate with virtual agents** ([EGVE 2023](https://diglib.eg.org/items/70e4bdde-687a-46c9-9cb7-9ffc92b4bb70/full)).

The most directly relevant result is Hinglish. In a Wizard-of-Oz study, bilingual users preferred chatbots that code-switch, and **preferred bots that mirrored their own code-switching pattern** (DOCUMENTED via secondary summary, [UW sociolab](https://depts.washington.edu/sociolab/labmembers/documents/eahn_sociolab_201030.pdf)). The CommonDost corpus of 439 Hindi–English human–machine dialogues was built to measure exactly this accommodation ([Parekh et al., CoNLL 2020](https://aclanthology.org/2020.conll-1.46)). Mirroring the *mix ratio* is supported. Mirroring accent is not: commercial accent "matching" is criticised as accentism ([UBC News 2026](https://news.ubc.ca/2026/05/accentism-for-profit-what-telus-is-getting-wrong-about-accents/)). Hostage-negotiation transcripts add a stability lesson. Success went with higher linguistic style matching, but the driver was **stability**, since failed negotiations swung widely in style ([Taylor & Thomas 2008](https://ncmr.lps.library.cmu.edu/article/id/93/), n = 9, correlational). That is the theoretical case for hysteresis on live switches.

For time-poor sellers, effort reduction is the principle. CEB's 97,000-customer study found that exceeding expectations barely moved loyalty, while low-effort service was **31% more likely to win repeat business** ([Salesforce summary](https://www.salesforce.com/ca/blog/making-effortless-customer-service-a-reality/)). Effort reduction, however, does not mean speed. In 1,380 telephone survey introductions, a **moderate ~3.5 words/s** won the most agreement, and both very fast and very slow delivery did worse. Short natural pauses beat perfectly fluent "too scripted" speech ([U. Michigan](https://news.umich.edu/persuasive-speech-the-way-we-um-talk-sways-our-listeners)). Gong's call analytics find that top sellers speak *slower* (~171 vs 182 wpm) and shift the prospect's tempo about 13% within three minutes, so they lead the tempo rather than mirror it ([Gong Labs](https://www.gong.io/resources/labs/elements-of-effective-sales-conversations/), figures via [SalesLion](https://saleslion.io/sales-statistics/71-words-per-minute-the-surprising-speech-speed-of-top-performers-who-speak-6-slower-than-their-peers/)). For confused listeners, slow speech is **not reliably better**. It helped only listeners with intact working memory and hurt those with severe impairment, while "clear speech" with more pauses improved recall ([Frontiers in Psychology 2023](https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2023.1119355)). Step-by-step delivery with confirmation is the documented pattern for listeners who struggle ([CMU GetGoing](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1909.01322)). The theory therefore converges: **answer rush with shorter content and an early ask, answer confusion with simpler, chunked wording, and keep pace near moderate in both cases**.

Data-driven practice learns the mapping from outcomes in three ways. The first is **mining top performers**: Cresta uses LLMs to find behaviours correlated with KPIs and coaches agents live, with vendor-reported gains of 2.1× revenue for agents who follow them ([Cresta](https://cresta.com/blog/4-sales-behaviors-proven-to-increase-revenue-and-drive-conversions)). Those claims carry obvious selection bias. The best peer-reviewed analogue is Brynjolfsson, Li and Raymond. In a staggered roll-out to 5,179 support agents, an assistant trained on top agents' conversations raised resolutions per hour by **14%, and by 34% for novices** ([NBER w31161](https://www.nber.org/papers/w31161)). The second is **pairing with randomised benchmarking**. Afiniti predicts outcomes for every caller–agent pair and measures lift by cycling the pairing ON and OFF ([Afiniti](https://www.afiniti.com/?p=18902); [patent US9692899](https://patents.google.com/patent/US9692899)). We found no peer-reviewed RCT of it. The third is **bandits or RL over strategies**, such as a contextual bandit that picks the message frame while an LLM writes it ([arXiv 2410.23371](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2410.23371)), and affect-adaptive spoken dialogue that raised task success over non-adaptive baselines ([Forbes-Riley & Litman, SIGDIAL 2012](https://www.superlectures.com/sigdial2012/adapting-to-multiple-affective-states-in-spoken-dialogue)).

All three methods need **variation in what the agent did** across comparable customers. PS07 has none in voice, tone or pace, and its bot versions were not randomised: **87.4% of a seller's consecutive calls used the same version, and 97% of Tamil Nadu sellers got one version** (PROVEN, [Data driven persona agent](Data%20driven%20persona%20agent.md)). IndiaMART's three static-personalisation trials also did not beat control (PROVEN). What PS07 *can* support is a Cresta-style mine of **bot moves inside the single script**. For example: "seller said busy → bot kept pitching → hang-up" versus "→ bot offered a slot → Call Later". Branches and LLM wording varied even under one style, but good sellers get good branches, so confounding is heavy (ASSUMED). There is one more warning from the LLM side. Chatbots drift their Agreeableness and Extraversion toward the user's persona over a conversation without being told to ([Xing et al., EMNLP 2025](https://aclanthology.org/2025.emnlp-main.875/)). Unpinned "adaptation" is uncontrolled sycophancy, so the persona spec must fix its levels explicitly.

## Robust defaults, dormant branches and prior-scaled thresholds do the translating

Sarvam fixes voice and pace per call. Our SDK spike showed that a per-call `text_to_speech_config` was ignored: female voice, pace 0.85 vs 1.25 and pitch ±0.2 all produced the same male voice. Language, opening line and variables work per call, and the plan travels in a directive the signal server re-sends each turn (PROVEN, [Data driven persona agent](Data%20driven%20persona%20agent.md)). That asymmetry dictates where a seller's history may act. **Irreversible settings move only when the prior is high and a mismatch is cheap. Reversible settings carry the strong bets.**

The pre-call layer is a **robust default**. A slightly brisk pace (1.0–1.05) costs a calm seller little, because ~3.5 words/s is already near optimal. A slow pace costs a rushed seller a lot. So VANI leans brisk by default and **never slows the base pace on a confusion prior**, because confusion is short-lived and wording can handle it live (ASSUMED, grounded in the Michigan and Frontiers results). The plan layer carries **pre-written contingency branches** in every call: `on_rush` (one-line value plus slot offer), `on_confusion` (identity and purpose in ≤ 8 words, then a yes/no check) and `on_english` (shift to English-leaning Hinglish). A high prior *promotes* a branch into the opening. For example, a prior rusher hears a time-box ("2 minute ka kaam hai") in the first line, while low-prior branches stay dormant but present (ASSUMED).

The live layer lowers **switch thresholds** as the prior rises. With a calibrated detector, VANI switches when the posterior `P(state | cue, p0)` exceeds τ. Bayes gives posterior odds = prior odds × likelihood ratio. A prior rusher (p0 = 0.31) therefore needs a cue about **1.7× weaker** than a base-rate seller (p0 = 0.21) to reach the same posterior. In practice: **one rush cue triggers crisp mode for a prior rusher; two cues are needed at base rate**. The threshold itself follows decision costs, `τ = cost(false switch) / (cost(false switch) + cost(missed switch))`. A missed rush risks a hang-up and a false crisp switch only shortens turns, so τ_rush is low (~0.35–0.4). A wrong language switch is jarring, so τ_English is higher (~0.6) (ASSUMED arithmetic). Hysteresis follows the style-stability evidence. Once crisp, VANI stays crisp unless the seller gives two or more long, engaged answers. Language switches at most once per call unless the seller switches back. Live evidence always wins: VANI never refuses to relax because "the prior says rushed".

No published voice-agent system documents prior-dependent thresholds. The mechanism rests on our recurrence data plus Bayes arithmetic, and **the detectors' likelihood ratios are unmeasured**. Our lexicon agreed with the PS07 evaluator at only κ ≈ 0.03 (PROVEN), so the LRs must be estimated on Jev-labelled turns before the thresholds mean anything.

## Designers keep three to five styles on shared dimensions, and data decides which survive

Designer-led practice is consistent and simple. Google's conversation-design guidance builds a persona from **4–6 key adjectives**. It casts the voice by an audition that rates candidate voices against those adjectives, and it advises against specifying gender or age, "because they almost never critically define or differentiate a persona" ([Google](https://developers.google.com/assistant/conversation-design/create-a-persona)). The 2026 state of the art turns that single persona into a small library. **Alexa+ ships Brief, Chill, Sweet and later Sassy**, all defined as levels on five shared dimensions: expressiveness (concise↔verbose), emotional openness, formality, directness and humour. The "same capable Alexa" underneath stays unchanged ([About Amazon](https://www.aboutamazon.com/news/devices/alexa-plus-personality-styles)). This is exactly the shape PS04 needs. **An agent persona is a vector of discrete levels on fixed dimensions, layered over a fixed fact and compliance layer that no persona can touch** (ASSUMED, matching PS04 FAQ Q5). Foundational work explains why consistency matters. Listeners apply social rules to synthetic voices, and a mismatch between voice personality and word personality lowers liking and trust ([Nass & Brave, *Wired for Speech*](https://mitpress.mit.edu/9780262640657/wired-for-speech/)). Sarvam's own Bulbul v4 catalogue is already organised in this designer style. Each voice carries adjectives, a pitch word, a pace word and a use case ([catalogue dump](../../raw/research/2026-10-07-sarvam-voice-catalogue.json), PROVEN).

Data-driven library building has four complementary tools. **Top-call mining** sets dimension levels from what winning calls did. Since VANI has no human reps to mine, the PS07 substitute is to compare meeting-fixed and non-meeting calls within each seller segment on VANI-side features that did vary, such as reply length, turns before the value statement and question count (ASSUMED). **Factorial, per-dimension learning** treats a persona as a combination of levels. Aampe runs per-user Thompson sampling with Beta posteriors over separate dimensions, including tone of voice ([Aampe docs](https://docs.aampe.com/docs/core-concepts)). Its 6.4M-user RCT showed gains of only +0.07% to +0.48% ([arXiv 2506.16429](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.16429v1)), a reminder that style effects are small. **Quality-diversity search** keeps the best candidate in each cell of a feature grid, with an LLM as the generator ([arXiv 2404.15794](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2404.15794); [arXiv 2504.14367](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2504.14367)). It is the natural way to propose new archetypes into empty cells without collapsing them into stereotypes, which LLM-generated persona populations are documented to do ([arXiv 2604.24698](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.24698)). **Live A/B and bandits** then refine. Because voice agents are multi-turn and non-deterministic, "a single run proves very little" ([Hamming](https://hamming.ai/resources/voice-agent-ab-testing-guide)).

On which voice attributes matter, the largest Indian study points away from gender. Anand et al. ran **120,000 pairwise comparisons by 1,900+ native raters across 10 Indic languages** and found that **expressiveness and intelligibility are the strongest predictors of TTS preference**, ahead of liveliness and voice quality. Comparisons were made within gender. Bulbul v3 Beta placed 4th of 7 with a 52% win rate and "comparatively lower expressiveness and liveliness" ([arXiv 2604.21481](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.21481)). Small 2025 studies find that gendered voices trigger trust patterns that follow stereotypes, warmth↔feminine and authority↔masculine ([IIS 2025](https://iacis.org/iis/2025/1_iis_2025_431-445.pdf)). This means **an optimiser rewarded on short-term response can learn and harden a stereotype**. UNESCO's 2019 report recommended ending female-by-default assistants for this reason ([CBS News](https://www.cbsnews.com/news/alexa-siri-other-artificial-intelligence-voice-assistants-gender-bias-unesco)). Industry routing systems draw the same line: Afiniti excludes protected-class data, and Genesys uses no gender or nationality ([Afiniti](https://www.afiniti.com/responsible-ai/how-it-works/); [Genesys](https://help.genesys.cloud/articles/use-of-ai-in-predictive-routing/)).

The starting library has **four Hindi/Hinglish archetypes, one English-led and one Tamil**. Every pair differs on at least two dimensions, at least one of them audible: speaker, pace, or brevity measured in words per turn. Voice and pace are baked into a published Sarvam agent version. Formality, warmth, brevity, language mix and plan style travel per call as variables and can switch live. Voices are drawn from the Bulbul v4 catalogue using Sarvam's own descriptors (PROVEN catalogue; audition still required).

Two numbers in this table deliberately differ from our earlier library draft. **The Patient Guide runs at pace 1.0, not 0.9**, because confusion is a short-lived state and slow speech is not reliably clearer, so simplicity belongs in wording. **The Crisp Closer runs at 1.05, not 1.1**, because top sellers win by saying less, not by speaking faster. Both corrections follow the translation evidence above.

**The Sarvam constraint shapes the library's size.** Every audible change (speaker, pace, pitch) requires a new published agent version, and the call chooses among them by `app_version` (PROVEN constraint). Primary voices only give **six versions for the demo**. Adding the alternate-gender voice for A–E brings it to **eleven**. That is manageable, while a full factorial of 2 genders × 3 paces × 3 formality × 3 warmth × 3 brevity × 3 plan styles (486 cells) is not. Voice and pace stay bundled, so their effects cannot be separated until Sarvam enables its on-start Code Tools, which are available only on request (DOCUMENTED). Every call must log `app_version_requested` and a `voice_observed` pitch check. The spike showed requested and spoken voice can differ, and median F0 separates the male (~85–155 Hz) and female (~165–255 Hz) bands (PROVEN technique).

The library is not the output, which PS04 FAQ Q1 forbids. It is the **audible base** that the per-seller generator composes on. Seller data sets the archetype by rule. The seller's own history, language, last objection and personalisation slots then produce a per-seller plan, opening and Hinglish ratio, each with a reason and a confidence. Two sellers in one archetype differ in plan, and sellers in different archetypes differ audibly (ASSUMED). The seller side has its own library: **seven behavioural seller types** mined from PS07 warm sellers (k-means K = 7, bootstrap ARI 0.868), with cold sellers handled by rules because they show no stable structure (ARI ≤ 0.44) (PROVEN, [PS07 library prototype](../research_notes/Data%20derived%20persona%20library/ps07_library_prototype.md)). **Seller types are the input segments; VANI archetypes are the output building blocks; the mapping between them is what has to be learned.** For the demo, the clearest audible contrast is A (male, brisk, terse) against B (female, warm, chunked) against E or F (a different language), so the pair differs on speaker, brevity *and* language at once.

## A seller-type × VANI-persona scoreboard turns assumptions into evidence

The day-zero mapping in the table above is a **hypothesis register**, not a policy proven by data. Each seller-type → archetype row enters as a GUESS-tier prior worth only ~10–25 pseudo-calls. This follows Pega's cold-start practice of weighting a starting propensity as 25 responses so that data overrides it quickly ([Pega Academy](https://academy.pega.com/node/47811)). The learning object is a scoreboard of Beta counts: **for each seller segment × VANI archetype, the number of calls and successes under a reward declared in advance**. A proposed reward is "meeting fixed in the call, or a positive next call within 14 days, with no DNC request", with a fixed join window and a default of 0 (ASSUMED). Afiniti-style ON/OFF benchmarking supplies the baseline. A fixed **10% of calls get the current default VANI persona** as a holdout. Thompson sampling then allocates the rest across the 2–3 plausible archetypes per segment with a floor share, so each call's selection probability is logged. A deterministic mapping would log propensity 1.0 everywhere and make the mapping permanently untestable. LLM bandit agents explore properly only when given summarised statistics rather than raw histories ([Krishnamurthy et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.15371)), so the Sarvam decision agent ranks candidates and code draws the arm (ASSUMED design, [Data driven persona agent](Data%20driven%20persona%20agent.md)).

The arithmetic sets the granularity. PS07's meeting-fixed rate is about 11.5% overall. Detecting a **3-point absolute lift (≈ 13% → 16%) at α = 0.05 and 80% power needs about 2·(1.96 + 0.84)²·p(1−p)/d² ≈ 2,000 calls per arm per segment**. A 5-point lift needs about 700 (ASSUMED arithmetic). With the seven warm seller types plus a cold segment, and three candidate archetypes each, a full read needs roughly **8 × 3 × 2,000 ≈ 48,000 explored calls**. That is weeks of traffic at IndiaMART's scale, but impossible for fine per-seller cells. So **the scoreboard learns archetype × coarse segment**, and personalisation within an archetype comes from rules and seller memory, not from experiments. Cheap, per-call dimensions such as formality, warmth and brevity get their own per-dimension posteriors, Aampe-style. Voice arms are compared only between published versions.

Explicit rules then refine the library (ASSUMED, after the minimum sample). **Keep** an archetype that is best, or within the credible interval of best, in at least one segment. **Tune** one that wins its segment but loses on a secondary metric, such as more NI or longer calls: change one dimension level, publish a minor version and run a paired test. **Drop** one whose posterior probability of being best is below 5% in every segment, or that fails a guardrail or compliance audit. **Split** one with high outcome variance that a seller feature explains, provided the children differ on an audible dimension. **Merge** two whose measured wpm, F0, speaker embedding and outcomes are indistinguishable. **Add** candidates that an LLM proposes into empty or weak quality-diversity cells, screened in simulation and by a judge, then given a 5–10% exploration share. Retired archetypes stay in the registry so that old decisions still resolve. Per-type posteriors carry over on splits (down-weighted) and pool on merges. Subgroup reporting is mandatory: an off-policy evaluation of a real LLM coaching agent found that a policy which raised average value **harmed specific subgroups** ([arXiv 2510.17173](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.17173)).

**The stereotype guard is part of the loop, not a slogan.** Seller gender, religion, caste and name-inferred ethnicity are never inputs. Language comes from observed speech, the PNS/zone proxy or a live switch, all functional rather than identity-based. Every archetype carries a male and a female voice where the catalogue allows. Gender is assigned by **balanced rotation and tested as its own arm**, never by "warm = female, authoritative = male". Allocation is audited per segment, with an alarm at more than 80% one-gender skew. Any arm whose lift comes only through a stereotyped mapping is stopped. Personas change delivery, never facts, prices or compliance text (PS04 FAQ Q5).

## Devil's advocate

The strongest objection is that **persona changes may not move outcomes at all**. IndiaMART's three static-personalisation trials failed to beat control. Microtargeting studies find negligible gains from demographic tailoring ([Hackenburg & Margetts, PNAS 2024](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC11181035/)). Aampe's gains, at 6.4M users, were fractions of a percentage point. If VANI's true style effect is one point rather than three, the required sample rises roughly ninefold to ~18,000 calls per arm per segment, and the scoreboard becomes a multi-quarter project. The honest hackathon claim is therefore fit, distinctness and live switching, not lift. Lift should be measured only against the holdout.

The translation table could also **cause the behaviour it predicts**. "NI history → short call with an easy exit" can produce the next NI. "Rushed → crisp" can teach a seller that VANI calls are throwaway. Only interleaved ON/OFF comparison detects such loops, and nothing in PS07 can. Similarly, **accommodation can backfire**. Rate entrainment lowered trust for some voice–language pairs, and mimicry gains often fail with agents. The table therefore mirrors code-mix ratio and brevity but never accent, slang or curtness. That line is a judgement, not a finding.

The **thin evidence base** deserves blunt statement. Confusion, English and regional priors rest on 19–68 transcribed pairs. Our lexicon's κ with the evaluator is ≈ 0.03. Transcripts cover only ~12.8K calls, biased toward engaged ones. Every threshold number in the table is an unsized design choice. No study anywhere tests delivery → outcome effects in Indian B2B phone calls. The nearest evidence is a US survey-call study and a Hinglish chatbot experiment. The "terse" trait may be partly an artefact of how people talk to bots.

The library itself has **structural weaknesses**. Within Hindi, pace 1.0 versus 1.05 may be inaudible, and Sarvam's "slow" and "brisk" labels are marketing text until measured. Tamil has three male narrator voices and no female or conversational option, so F cannot be gender-balanced and its distinctness must come from wording. The 2026 Indian TTS study rated Bulbul v3 low on expressiveness, the attribute that matters most, and v4 is untested there. Version sprawl caps experimentation at a handful of voice arms. Judges may still read "seller → archetype" as the fixed library that FAQ Q1 rejects, unless the demo leads with the per-seller spec and its deltas.

Finally, **exploration on real sellers is deliberately non-optimal and needs a business ruling**, and the whole learning loop pays off only if live VANI traffic runs through this system after the event. Bandits, uplift models and Afiniti-style pairing are outside PS04's two-day scope and should be presented as roadmap. What *is* in scope, and cheap, is the decision record: the as-of seller snapshot, the archetype chosen, its propensity and a `decision_id` joined to outcomes. Without it, the loop can never start.

## Conclusion

The reframing that matters is that **seller history is a probability, not a personality**. Treating "rushed" as a 31% lean rather than a label changes what VANI should do with it. It should not slow or speed the whole call. It should start safe, keep the right escape branch one sentence away and switch sooner when the seller confirms the risk. The same logic explains why the library must stay small and built from dimensions. Irreversible choices such as voice and pace deserve few, well-audited options, while reversible choices such as wording, brevity and plan carry the personalisation.

The second shift is about what counts as evidence. PS07 is large but structurally silent on VANI's style, because one voice cannot reveal which voice works. Every mapping from seller type to archetype is a registered guess with a predicted direction, a gender-balanced voice pair and a minimum sample. The scoreboard either confirms or retires it. A team that logs its first explored call during the hackathon will hold, a few months later, the only dataset that can answer the question this report can answer only with theory.
